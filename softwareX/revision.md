# Academic Works Using or Citing Ener-Habitat (IER-UNAM): Evidence Base for a SoftwareX Submission

## TL;DR
- **Ener-Habitat has a clearly documented academic footprint of at least ~20 verifiable scholarly works** (graduate theses, peer-reviewed articles, and conference papers, in Spanish and English) that either ran simulations with the tool or cited it — anchored by its own foundational publications in *Solar Energy* (2016) and *Energy Procedia* (2014). This is more than sufficient to justify academic relevance for a SoftwareX paper.
- **The strongest justification is the body of works that USED the tool in their methodology** — graduate theses at UNAM and other Mexican universities plus peer-reviewed case studies spanning walls, roofs, historic monuments, PCM walls, and social/remittance housing across multiple institutions (IER-UNAM, Universidad de Sonora, Universidad Autónoma de Coahuila, UADY, TecNM/CENIDET, UAGro, IPN-Oaxaca, UABC).
- The tool also serves as a **benchmark/reference** in the Mexican building-science literature (cited when authors discuss national time-dependent heat-transfer tools versus the NOM-008/NOM-020 standards). It was created by researchers from six Mexican academic institutions with CONACYT–SENER funding, and its developer lead reports **more than 2,600 registered users and an average of more than 3,600 evaluations per month**, reinforcing practical uptake beyond citation counts.

## Key Findings
- The foundational software paper is: **Barrios, G.; Casas-Arredondo, J.M.; Huelsz, G.; Rojas, J. (2016)**, "Ener-Habitat: An online numerical tool to evaluate the thermal performance of homogeneous and non-homogeneous envelope walls/roofs," *Solar Energy* 131: 296–304 (DOI: 10.1016/j.solener.2015.12.017).
- Two companion/method papers reinforce the citation fingerprint: the conference article **"Ener-Habitat: A Cloud Computing Numerical Tool..."** (*Energy Procedia* 57, 2014: 2042–2051) and the **EHLS method paper** (Huelsz, Barrios, Rojas 2016, *Applied Thermal Engineering* 102: 1019–1023). Per the *Energy Procedia* cost-benefit paper, "Ener-Habitat was created by researchers from six academic institutions in Mexico and was funded by the National Council for Science and Technology and the Ministry of Energy of Mexico."
- Works USING the tool span multiple institutions and building types (housing envelopes, historic monuments, PCM walls, remittance housing, disaster-relief social housing).
- Works CITING only tend to be comparative building-science papers that reference Ener-Habitat as a Mexican time-dependent heat-transfer tool or a point of comparison against NOM standards, or that motivate new/competing tools.

## Recommendations
1. **Lead the "impact/relevance" section with Group A (works that USED the tool)**, emphasizing the diversity of institutions and building types: reinforced-concrete vs. hollow-block walls (Molar-Orozco & Huelsz 2017), historic monuments (UADY 2024), remittance housing (IPN-Oaxaca 2024), PCM walls (Moreles et al. 2018), disaster-relief housing (UAGro 2024), plus graduate theses at UNAM, TecNM, and UABC.
2. **Cite the foundational *Solar Energy* (2016) and *Energy Procedia* (2014) papers as canonical references**, and use the EHLS *Applied Thermal Engineering* (2016) paper to establish methodological novelty and its independent international citation trail.
3. **Present a two-column table (USED vs. CITED)** with type and language tags; before final submission, verify the three flagged leads (Espinosa 2017; CENIDET project; the specific IER theses and the Oaxaca adobe/bahareque thesis tool split) via full text and reclassify as needed.
4. **Report the developer-stated adoption metrics** — "more than 2,600 users… more than 3,600 evaluations per month" (attributed to Guillermo Barrios Valle in *El Universal*) — as complementary evidence of practical uptake, and note the tool was built by six Mexican institutions with CONACYT–SENER funding.
5. **Thresholds that would change the framing:** if full-text checks confirm ≥15 independent (non-developer) works that USED the tool, foreground real-world adoption as the headline claim; if most confirmed uses turn out to be developer-authored, instead frame relevance around the validated EHLS method and its third-party citations, presenting tool usage as a growing but earlier-stage indicator.

## Caveats
- Google Scholar "cited by" pages could not be programmatically enumerated, so total citation counts are approximate; **this list is a floor, not a ceiling** — a full-text Scholar sweep will likely surface further Spanish-language theses.
- Several works co-authored by the developer group (Barrios, Huelsz, Rojas, Casas-Arredondo, Moreles) demonstrate the tool but are **not independent third-party evidence**; they are flagged as DEVELOPER and should be presented as such.
- A few items (Espinosa 2017; the CENIDET project; specific IER theses; the Oaxaca adobe/bahareque thesis) are **leads requiring full-text confirmation** of whether Ener-Habitat was used in methodology versus merely cited.
- **Developer-reported user statistics** (>2,600 users; >3,600 monthly evaluations) come from a 2016 press interview and should be attributed to the developer rather than presented as independently audited; a note that these buildings' construction dates (2012 launch) mean current figures may differ is warranted.
- One student coursework item (Universidad del Valle de México, on Studocu) uses the tool as a class exercise; it is non-peer-reviewed and was excluded from the numbered list as low academic weight.
